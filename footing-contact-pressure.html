How the pressure is calculated

The footing is rigid, so it settles as a plane, and the soil is treated as a bed of springs that can push but not pull. Where the whole footing stays in contact, the pressure is the familiar linear distribution, with x and y measured from the centre of the footing:

p = R/A + My x / Iy + Mx y / Ix, My = Σ Fi xi, Mx = Σ Fi yi

No part of the footing is in tension while the resultant stays inside the kern, which for a rectangle is the rhombus 6|ex|/l + 6|ey|/b ≤ 1.

Outside the kern, the pressure is still a plane, p = a + cx x + cy y, but it acts only where it is positive. The three unknowns must balance the resultant force and both moments over the contact area, which itself depends on the unknowns. The tool solves this by iteration, as in the chapter: start with the whole footing in contact, drop the part where the pressure is negative, solve the three equilibrium equations again over the remaining area, and repeat until the contact area stops changing. Here the integrals over the contact polygon are evaluated exactly, and each step is checked so that it always moves towards the solution.

If the resultant falls outside the footing, no contact pressure can balance the loads and the footing overturns.
