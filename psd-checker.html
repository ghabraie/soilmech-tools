Particle-size distribution checker

Work through a sieve analysis yourself: fill in the mass passing and percent finer for each sieve, plot the curve, and read the parameters from it. Then check your work. Mistakes come back as questions pointing at the first thing to revisit; hints and the full solution are there when you need them.

Enter one sieve per line as “size in mm, mass retained in g”, from the largest sieve down, and finish with “pan, mass”. Optionally add hydrometer readings as “h, size in µm, mass finer in g”.

Paste or type the data: the first column is the particle size in mm, followed by one column of percent finer for each soil (up to six). A header row with the soil names is optional. Columns can be separated by commas, tabs (as when pasting from a spreadsheet), semicolons or spaces. Leave a cell empty if a soil was not tested on that sieve.

How the checker works

Each row of the table is checked against your own previous row, so one slip does not turn the whole column red: the mass passing a sieve should equal the mass passing the sieve above minus the mass retained on it, and the percent finer should equal that mass divided by the total mass of the sample.

The reference curve joins the data points with straight lines on the semi-log plot, so sizes are interpolated on a logarithmic scale: D = D1(D2/D1)(P − P1)/(P2 − P1). A smooth curve drawn by hand gives slightly different values, so sizes read from the curve are accepted within ±12%, and percentages within ±2.5%. Cu and Cc are checked against your own D values.

If less than 10% of the soil is finer than the smallest size tested, D10 cannot be read from the data. Tick “not determinable” in that case, and think about what you could do instead.
