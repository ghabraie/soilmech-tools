How the checker works

The checker classifies the soil itself, following the rules in the chapter, and compares your answer with each decision in turn: coarse or fine, gravel or sand, the amount of fines, the grading, and the type and plasticity of the fines. It tells you about the first decision that does not match, as a question. A hint then shows the numbers behind that decision. The full solution is available after your first attempt.

Group names are compared by their parts, so word order, capitals, hyphens and commas do not matter: “poorly graded sand with silt and gravel” and “Poorly-graded SAND with gravel and silt” are treated the same. In AS 1726 the primary component should still be written in capitals when you write it up.

Random soils are generated well away from every boundary in the classification rules, so each has one clear answer. Percentages are accepted within ±1 (±2 for values read from a graph).
