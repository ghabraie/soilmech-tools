How the flow net is calculated

The permeable layer is divided into a fine grid of square cells, and the steady-state seepage equation is solved for the total head at every node by the finite difference (finite volume) method. The upstream and downstream ground surfaces are equipotential lines (h = H1 and h = H2, with the ground surface as the datum); the base of the dam, the sheet piles and the impermeable layer below are flow lines; the far ends of the model are placed far enough away to have little effect.

The equipotential lines are drawn at head intervals of δh = H/Nd. The flow lines are contours of the stream function, drawn at intervals of k′δh per metre, so that each flow channel carries the same flow and the cells are curvilinear squares. The number of flow channels, Nf, is generally not a whole number: the last channel is a part channel, just as when a flow net is sketched by hand. The rate of seepage is q = k′H Nf/Nd, and the shape factor Nf/Nd does not change with the number of potential drops you choose.

For anisotropic soils, the problem is solved on the transformed section (horizontal dimensions multiplied by √(kz/kx)) with k′ = √(kxkz), and the flow net is then mapped back to the natural section, where its cells are no longer square.

The exit gradient is the vertical hydraulic gradient at the downstream ground surface. Next to a sheet pile or the toe of a dam it depends on the grid size, so treat it as an estimate.
