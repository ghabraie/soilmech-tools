Compaction curve plotter

Enter the moisture content and dry density (or dry unit weight, or the mass of wet soil in the mould) of each compacted specimen. The tool fits a smooth curve as AS 1289.5.1.1 describes, with its wet leg parallel to an air voids line, finds the maximum dry density and optimum moisture content at its peak, and draws the zero-air-void curve. Click a point on the plot to leave it out of the fit. Add more soils to compare their curves.

How the curve and the optimum are found

AS 1289.5.1.1 (standard compaction) and AS 1289.5.2.1 (modified) ask for the dry densities to be plotted against moisture content and a smooth curve of best fit to be drawn through the points, with the wet leg of the curve tending parallel to the air voids line. The maximum dry density (MDD) and optimum moisture content (OMC) are the coordinates of the peak. The standard also allows a curve-fitting program that plots through the points, such as a cubic spline, to locate the peak.

AS method (automatic) uses the first approach whenever the particle density is known and at least four points are in use; otherwise it uses a cubic spline. The best-fit curve joins a dry leg (a straight line, or a gentle curve when there are six or more points) smoothly to a curve of constant air voids, ρd = ρs(1 − A)/(1 + wρs/ρw), so its wet leg is parallel to an air voids line by construction. The dry leg, the air voids content A of the wet leg and the roundness of the peak are found by least squares; the air voids line that the wet leg follows is drawn dotted. Because it is a best fit, the peak may sit slightly below the highest measured point. The cubic spline passes through every point, so it follows their scatter too. Polynomial fits are offered for comparison.

The curve is drawn only between the driest and wettest points used; it is never extrapolated. If the highest point of the curve is at one end, the optimum is not bracketed by the data and more specimens are needed on that side.

The particle density ρs (numerically equal to Gs in t/m³) is best measured (AS 1289.3.5.1). Estimate applies the rough check in the note to AS 1289.5.1.1: take the dry density of the wettest point, assume 2% air voids, and find the particle density that puts a point 1% wetter on that air voids line. With ρs given, the zero-air-void curve is ρd = ρs/(1 + wρs/ρw); a point above it is impossible and points to an error in the data or in ρs.

When the mass of wet soil in the mould is entered, the dry density is ρd = M/[V(1 + w)]. Dry unit weights are converted using γd = 9.81ρd (kN/m³, with ρd in t/m³).

The checks below the results are good practice for a reliable curve, not a substitute for the test standard; check the standard for the number of specimens, the moisture increments and how results are reported.
